# R06: paths, glyphs and the Glass Wood props

Reviewer method: rendered `lh-door`, `tree-close`, `dock`, `stone-close`, `gw-court` and `gw-altar` at 1600x900 and looked at them. Credit first: the dock (varied boards, nail heads, rope-coiled posts, wet boards at the seaward end), the lighthouse door (grain, hex-headed bolts, strap hinges), the warm ground bounce, and the mid-distance conifers (tiered drooping sprays that now read as conifers from the dock) are all clear improvements. Everything below is a visible defect in a frame. Same rules as before: triage into TASKS.md, before/after frames Read and compared, revert parts with no visible gain, selftests and bots green, commit at each pausing point, INSPECT.md updated, +1.0 ms budget, story ending left to the user.

## Defects, in priority order

1. **The paths still show a rectangular lattice** (`stone-close` lower left; `gw-altar` the whole path). The pebble look comes from a thresholded value-noise `pebble` term, so the pebbles are blurry rectangles. This is the surface the player walks on most, and the new footsteps now say "crunch", so it should look like it. Replace it (Varrow earth path and the Glass Wood path) with a **Worley/cellular cobble pattern**: rounded pebbles of varied size (use two cell scales), a dark gap between neighbours, per-pebble tone and roughness, a height value per pebble that drives the bump normal (and a touch of AO in the gaps), a few larger stones, sand and soil filling the gaps, and the existing wear/edge blending into the grass. Band-limit by `fwidth` as before. For the Glass Wood use the same structure in its palette (pale blue-violet stones with a faint glassy sheen). Accept: before/after `stone-close` and `gw-altar`: rounded pebbles, no axis-aligned shapes.
2. **The resonance stone glyphs are flat blue stickers** (`stone-close`). They are the core interactive object of the game and the player touches them. Make them **carved channels**: cut the glyph shapes into the stone (a recessed groove with a bevelled edge, 1 to 3 cm deep), put the emissive material at the bottom of the groove so the stone edge occludes it and shows depth at an angle, add a thin lit rim, and let the glow spill softly onto the stone around it (a small blurred emissive halo, a fake local light, since a general point-light pass was not worth it). Keep the existing pulse, lit state, tone and puzzle hooks. There is also a thin vertical bar near the bottom of the stone face that looks like a stray rectangle: check what it is and remove or integrate it. Accept: before/after `stone-close`, plus a lit-state frame.
3. **The journal page is a plain white card** (`stone-close`, bottom right). It is the object that carries the story. Give it paper character: slightly off-white warm tone, faint fibre grain, curled and uneven edges (displace the quad), ruled or handwritten-looking ink lines in a darker sepia (procedural strokes, no text needed), a gentle fold, a soft contact shadow, and a small pebble or shell weighting a corner. Keep its hover pulse and interaction. Accept: before/after frame.
4. **Glass Wood puzzle props are bare boxes** (`gw-court`, `gw-altar`): the mural is a dark rectangle with flat white bars; the mirror stands are flat white and blue rectangles on thin posts; the court floor is a flat grid; the altar is a plain disc. This is the same uneven-fidelity problem as the Varrow dock was. Bring them up to the lighthouse standard in the Glass Wood's own material language (the world is glass, crystal, cold stone and light): mirrors as real **mirrors** (framed in iron or pale metal, a low-roughness metallic surface reflecting the sky and stars via the sky LUT, with a visible bevel), stands with a pivot collar, a base plate and a visible rotation mark or detent ring, the mural as a carved and inlaid plate (recessed bars with emissive inlay like item 2, a frame, chiselled edge), the court floor as inlaid slabs with joint lines and a worn centre, the altar with steps, a carved rim and a faint inner glow. Reuse the new material ids and builders. Keep every puzzle hook and collider. Accept: before/after `gw-court`, `gw-chimes` and `gw-altar`.
5. **Glass Wood crystal trees are the weakest asset left.** Pale flat tubes with thin straight branches and flat shard "leaves"; nothing like the crystal veined trunks the design describes. Rebuild them like the conifers were: a faceted trunk (polygonal, slightly twisted, with horizontal growth bands), branches that fork and taper, and crowns of crystal clusters (groups of 3 to 7 elongated hexagonal prisms with pointed tips at varied angles) using the existing refractive glass pass, with an emissive vein running up the trunk and faint colour variation per crystal (teal to violet). Per-tree variation, three LODs, the same budget discipline. Accept: before/after `gw-arrival` and `gw-overview`.
6. **Grass is still a uniform carpet in the Glass Wood** (`gw-court`): even density and height with no clumps, bare patches or taller tufts like Varrow got. Reuse the Varrow grass variation, with a world-specific palette; add a few glowing small flowers or crystal tufts near the path to give the eye something. Accept: before/after `gw-court`.
7. **Smaller:** the `lh-door` view is now tightly cropped against the door (the 0.8 m view shows only planks and straps, with no arch visible); retarget it slightly back and up so the arch, jambs and threshold are in frame, since it is the acceptance view for the surround. The faint ring contour on the lighthouse's lower white band (you flagged it) can be removed by raising that blob mask's threshold or replacing it with the Worley pattern from R04; please do that.

## Deliberately not asked

Needle-level conifers. The crowns are now stylised drooping sprays, and the next step (many individual needles via alpha-tested cards) would need alpha-to-coverage or MSAA to avoid crawling edges. That trade-off, and whether stylised is the right look, is a decision for the user, not for you or me; list it in INSPECT.md as a question. Likewise no further changes to sun, fog or exposure.

## Review status

Items 1 to 4 are the most valuable: they are the things the player walks on and touches. Remaining gaps after this round will mostly be matters of taste or art direction (stylised vs photoreal conifers, how thick the haze is, how dark the shade is) that need the user's eye. I expect the next review to either find a small number of concrete defects or conclude the loop.
