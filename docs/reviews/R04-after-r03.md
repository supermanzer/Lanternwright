## Defects, in priority order

1. **Stone pitting reads as pixel art** (`lh-door`, `lh-base`). The pits are small axis-aligned L, T and step shapes, the signature of a thresholded value-noise lattice. Real pitting is rounded, varied in size, clustered, and deeper in some blocks than others. Build the pit mask from cellular (Worley) or gradient noise with a smooth threshold (`smoothstep` over a band, not a hard cut), rotate the noise per octave (or per block) so nothing aligns to the grid, vary size over 3x, and gate density per block (some blocks nearly clean, some heavily pitted). Same fix for the paint flaking: the pale patches in the red band (`lh-mid`, centre) include a clear **plus-shaped** blotch, another lattice artifact. Flaking should cluster at band edges, under the string courses and along drip lines, expose white primer or stone with a thin dark rust rim, and be irregular. Accept: no axis-aligned shapes visible in `lh-door`, `lh-base` or `lh-mid`.
2. **Masonry is too machine-made.** Blocks are identical flat rectangles in constant-height courses with a uniform bevel (like tiles). Add per-block face undulation (low-frequency height variation so each face is slightly convex or dished), irregular chiselled edge width, per-block course-height jitter (0.26 to 0.46 m) with the occasional through-stone or half-block, larger per-block hue and value variation (warm and cool greys, an odd rust-stained block), and slightly irregular joint width. The lower shaft is *meant* to be rough-hewn granite. Accept: `lh-base` reads as hand-cut stone, not a tile wall.
3. **Teal cast in shade** (`lh-base`): shaded granite goes blue-green slate because the only fill light is the cool sky. This is the one place the missing **warm ground bounce** (backlog B6, not done) is plainly visible. Add it: in the irradiance lookup, tint the lower hemisphere with the sun-lit ground (ground albedo x sun colour x sun elevation, roughly 0.2 to 0.35 of the direct sun irradiance), so shade is warmer and lighter near the ground. Also check `tree-close`, where the ground under the tree is nearly black. Accept: before/after `lh-base` and `tree-close` show warm, readable shade; check `dock`, `shore` and `gw-arrival` for no regressions.
4. **Door surround is stacked boxes, not an arch** (`lh-door`): chamfered blocks with pyramidal "pillow" faces, misaligned offsets, a few intersecting slabs, and no visible arch or keystone in the framed shot. Build it with the new `arch` builder: tapered voussoirs radiating from the arch centre, a keystone, alternating long and short quoin stones on the jambs, a simple moulded label above, a worn threshold. Drop the pyramidal bevel (a flat chamfer is fine). There is also a thin vertical bar above the ring handle that looks like a stray rectangle: check what it is. Accept: `lh-door` shows a real arched surround.
5. **Conifers read as broadleaf trees** (`tree-close`): the crown clumps are large rounded flat leaf shapes with hard polygonal edges, like ginkgo or maple leaves, not conifer boughs. Conifer character is layered, drooping, ragged, pointed sprays. Make each clump an elongated, slightly drooping fan along its branch (long axis outward, flattened vertically, tapering to a point), use many more and smaller ones (60 to 120 near LOD), jitter the outline with noise so it is ragged, shade the outer facets smoothly (smooth normals bent outward from the crown centre; the hard flat facets on the silhouette are the main thing that says "low-poly leaf"), and keep the darker interior and lit tips. Also remove the dark collar ring on the trunk at the base of the crown, and give the base some structure (root flare is there; add moss or a litter ring). Accept: before/after `tree-close` and the mid-distance `dock` frame: crowns read as conifer tiers.
6. **Ground and grass under the tree** (`tree-close`): the near ground is smooth near-black olive with no detail, and the grass blades are a few big flat triangles, very sparse and out of scale next to the tree. Add forest-floor variation under conifers (needle litter and duff: a darker, finer-grained, warmer-brown material in a radius around each trunk, a few cones and twigs as clutter), raise the density of blades and make them thinner and a little curved. Accept: before/after `tree-close`.
7. **Uneven fidelity: the lighthouse now outshines everything near it.** The dock (the first thing the player sees; `dock` frame) is still flat brown planks with black gaps and octagonal posts. The new `wood` material already exists and looks great on the door. Apply it: dock planks with grain, per-plank warp and tone, nail heads, weathering (silvered tops, darker wet lower boards), cleats, rope on the mooring posts, weed and barnacle bands on the piles at the waterline. Then bring the other hand-built props up to the same standard with the right materials: the lantern posts (iron and stone, glowing glass), the three resonance stones (basalt or dark stone with the glyph inlay, chisel marks, lichen at the base; they are the core of the puzzle and the player will touch them), the path markers and the journal-page stands. Accept: before/after `dock`, plus a close view of each resonance stone.
8. **Let the player actually stand at the wall.** The closest approach is 1.65 m because the lighthouse collider was not changed. "How it would feel under my fingers" needs about 0.5 m. Fit the collider to the real wall (tower radius at height plus `Player.radius`) and let the player climb the plinth steps (each is within `maxStep`). Re-run the bots and the lighthouse-pad tests. Accept: `lh-door` reproducible from 0.8 m by walking there; the stair-step plinth is walkable without snagging.
9. **Smaller points:** the tower's lower white band in `lh-mid` has a faint ring-shaped contour (a thresholded blob outline), same cause as item 1; the copper roof's dark patches (you already noted) could be eased with a slightly lighter reflection fallback (a dim roughness-biased sky tint) rather than pure sky; the stone lantern in `tree-close` is good, keep it as the quality reference.
